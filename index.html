Mastering the "Prestazione Occasionale" in Italy

If you are an expat or a foreign resident in Italy, the Ritenuta d'Acconto (Withholding Tax) is your gateway to legal earnings without the overhead of a VAT number (Partita IVA). It is the standard way to get paid for "spot" jobs, such as consulting, teaching, or creative services.

1. Eligibility: Can you use this?

To use this regime legally under Art. 67 del TUIR, your work must be occasional—meaning it lacks "habituality." If you perform the same task every day for the same client, the authorities may expect you to open a Partita IVA. However, for one-off projects or sporadic collaborations, this is the perfect legal shortcut.

2. The €5,000 "Exemption" Limit

As long as your total annual income from occasional work stays below €5,000 (Gross), you do not need to pay social security contributions (INPS). Important: If you exceed this limit, you must register for the Gestione Separata INPS, which involves an additional tax of roughly 33%, usually split between you and the client.

3. The €2.00 "Marca da Bollo" Rule

If your invoice/receipt is for an amount greater than €77.47, Italian law requires a €2.00 revenue stamp (Marca da Bollo).

How to do it: Buy the stamp at any Tabaccheria. The date on the stamp must be the same or earlier than the date on your receipt. Use our generator to input the ID code from the stamp to make it official.

4. Working for Non-Italian Clients

If your client is located outside of Italy (e.g., a US or UK company), they cannot act as a "Sostituto d'Imposta." This means you will receive 100% of the gross amount. However, you are still legally required to declare this income in your Italian tax return (Modello Redditi) and pay the relevant IRPEF taxes later.
