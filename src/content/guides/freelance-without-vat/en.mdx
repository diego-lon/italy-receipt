You don't need a VAT number (*Partita IVA*) to get paid for a one-off job in Italy. If the work is genuinely occasional, you can bill it as *prestazione occasionale* (occasional self-employment) with a signed receipt instead of an invoice. This guide covers who can use it, how tax and social security work in 2026, and when you need a Partita IVA.

## What prestazione occasionale is

*Prestazione occasionale*, formally *lavoro autonomo occasionale*, is work that you do:

- **personally**, without a business structure behind you;
- **without subordination**: the client doesn't direct your hours and methods the way an employer would;
- **without coordination** with the client's organisation;
- **without habituality or continuity**: it's a one-off or sporadic job, not your regular trade.

The civil-law basis is art. 2222 of the Italian Civil Code (*contratto d'opera*). For tax purposes the income is classed as *redditi diversi* ("other income") under art. 67, paragraph 1, letter l) of the TUIR (DPR 917/1986).

Because the activity isn't habitual, it falls outside the scope of VAT (art. 5 DPR 633/1972). In practice that means:

1. you don't need a Partita IVA;
2. you never add VAT to your price;
3. you don't issue an electronic invoice through the SDI system: a paper or PDF receipt (*ricevuta*) is the right document.

Typical examples are a one-off translation, logo design or event shoot.

<Callout type="info" title="Not the same as PrestO or Libretto Famiglia">
PrestO and the Libretto Famiglia (art. 54-bis DL 50/2017) are a separate system for small jobs, paid through the INPS platform with its own limits. This guide covers only prestazione occasionale paid against a receipt.
</Callout>

## Who can use it

Anyone with an Italian *codice fiscale* (tax code) can work this way, including:

- **students**;
- **employees**, if their contract allows it; public employees need prior authorisation (art. 53 D.Lgs. 165/2001);
- **pensioners**;
- **unemployed people**: if you receive unemployment benefits such as NASpI, ask INPS or a *patronato* how occasional income affects them;
- **foreigners**, whether they live in Italy or abroad. See [prestazione occasionale for foreigners](/en/prestazione-occasionale-foreigners/) for residency, permits and the 30% rule.

## Occasional or habitual: when you need a Partita IVA

The test is the **nature** of the work, not how much you earn. There is no legal annual income cap for occasional work.

Signs that your work is no longer occasional:

- you do the same kind of job regularly, for the same or different clients;
- one client relies on you on an ongoing basis, for example every month;
- you have built an organisation around it, such as premises, staff or a structured business.

If your activity becomes habitual or organised, you need a Partita IVA even if you earn less than €5,000. On the other hand, a single, genuinely one-off job can stay occasional even above €5,000. The [€5,000 limit guide](/en/5000-euro-limit-italy/) explains where that figure really comes from.

## How you get paid: four common cases

What you take home depends on who the client is and where you are tax resident. The first three rows assume you are an Italian tax resident; amounts exclude the €2 stamp.

| Who pays you | Italian withholding | You receive on a €1,000 job |
|---|---|---|
| Italian business, entity or professional with a VAT number | 20% *ritenuta d'acconto* (an advance on your income tax) | €800 |
| Private individual | None | €1,000 |
| Foreign client with no Italian presence | None in Italy | €1,000 |
| You are non-resident and the client is an Italian business | 30% final withholding, unless a tax treaty reduces it | €700 |

**Italian business clients** (companies, associations and other entities, plus businesses and professionals with a VAT number) are *sostituti d'imposta* (withholding agents). They deduct 20% from the gross amount and pay it to the State for you (art. 25 DPR 600/1973). Clients under the flat-rate regime (*regime forfettario*) are not required to withhold.

**Private individuals** are not withholding agents: you receive the full amount and declare it yourself.

**Foreign clients** without an Italian presence don't apply Italian withholding, but as an Italian tax resident you still declare the income, because residents are taxed on their worldwide income.

**Non-residents** paid by an Italian withholding agent for work done in Italy have 30% withheld as a final tax (art. 25, paragraph 2, DPR 600/1973). A double tax treaty may reduce or remove it, usually against a certificate of tax residence and the relevant Italian Revenue Agency form.

<Callout type="warning" title="15% and 5% are not withholding rates">
You may see 15% or 5% quoted online. Those are the flat-rate regime's substitute tax rates for Partita IVA holders, not rates for occasional work.
</Callout>

## The €5,000 INPS threshold, briefly

€5,000 is a social security threshold, not a cap on earnings (art. 44, paragraph 2, DL 269/2003):

- it is counted across **all** your clients, per **calendar year**, based on the gross fees actually paid to you in the year;
- up to €5,000, no INPS contributions are due;
- above it, contributions are due only on the excess: 33.72% in 2026, or 24% if you are a pensioner or already insured with another mandatory scheme, for example as an employee (INPS circular n. 8/2026);
- the client pays two thirds and you pay one third, which the client withholds from your payment;
- you must register with the INPS *Gestione Separata* and tell your clients how much you have already received that year.

Contributions are collected through clients that are withholding agents; if you pass €5,000 mainly with private or foreign clients, check your position with a CAF or patronato. The [€5,000 threshold guide](/en/5000-euro-limit-italy/) has a worked example.

## Stamp duty: the €2 marca da bollo

Receipts for more than €77.47 need a €2 revenue stamp (*marca da bollo*), because the amount isn't subject to VAT:

- buy the stamp at a tobacconist (*tabaccheria*) on or before the date of the receipt;
- stick it on the original and write its 14-digit serial number on the receipt;
- you can charge the €2 to the client, and that amount is not subject to withholding.

## What your client has to do

If the client is a withholding agent (an Italian business, entity or professional), it must:

- pay the withholding to the State with an F24 form (tax code 1040) by the 16th of the following month;
- give you a *Certificazione Unica* (CU) by 16 March of the following year, showing what it paid and withheld;
- above €5,000, pay the INPS contributions: its two thirds plus your third.

Since 2022, clients operating as businesses (*imprenditori*) must also send a prior notice (*comunicazione preventiva*) to the Labour Inspectorate (INL) before the occasional work starts (art. 14 D.Lgs. 81/2008, as amended by DL 146/2021). Fines range from €500 to €2,500 per worker. Private individuals don't have this obligation.

## Your tax return

Occasional income is taxable whatever the amount and whoever paid you, and the 20% withholding is only an advance. In your yearly tax return (730 or Redditi PF):

- you report what you **received** during the year (cash basis);
- you can deduct expenses specifically incurred to produce that income (art. 71 TUIR);
- in the 730 it goes in Quadro D, line D5 (code 2); in Redditi PF, in Quadro RL;
- the withholding shown on your CUs is credited against your IRPEF (income tax), so depending on your total income you may get a refund or owe more.

Private and foreign clients don't issue a CU, so your own receipts are your record. Keep a copy of every receipt for at least five years.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding VAT or calling the document a *fattura*.** Without a Partita IVA you issue a ricevuta, with a note that the amount is outside the scope of VAT.
- **Treating €5,000 as a tax-free allowance.** It only concerns INPS: income tax applies from the first euro.
- **Forgetting the €2 stamp** on receipts above €77.47.
- **Using receipts for a regular, ongoing activity.** Habitual work needs a Partita IVA.
- **Not declaring income from private or foreign clients** just because nothing was withheld.

## FAQ

### Can I do occasional work if I already have a job?

Yes, if your employment contract allows it: check for exclusivity or non-compete clauses. Public employees need prior authorisation. Above €5,000, the reduced INPS rate of 24% normally applies, because as an employee you are already insured with another mandatory scheme.

### Is there a maximum I can earn without a Partita IVA?

There is no fixed maximum. What matters is whether the work is occasional: habitual or organised work needs a Partita IVA at any income level, while a genuinely one-off job can stay occasional above €5,000.

### Do I pay tax if the client withheld nothing?

Yes. Private and foreign clients don't withhold, but the income is still taxable: you declare it in your tax return and IRPEF is calculated on your total income.

### Do I have to issue an electronic invoice?

No. Prestazione occasionale is outside the e-invoicing system (SDI). You issue a signed receipt, on paper or as a PDF.
