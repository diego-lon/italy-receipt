You've probably read that you can earn "up to €5,000 a year" without a VAT number (*Partita IVA*). It's the most common misunderstanding about *prestazione occasionale*, Italy's occasional self-employed work. €5,000 is neither a cap on your earnings nor a tax-free allowance: it's the point above which INPS social security contributions start. This guide explains what really changes at €5,000, how contributions are calculated in 2026 and what you need to do.

## The myth: a €5,000 income cap

Two different rules often get mixed up:

- **Whether you need a Partita IVA** depends on the nature of your work, not on the amount. Work that is habitual, continuous or organised needs a Partita IVA; genuinely occasional work doesn't. There is no legal annual income cap for occasional work.
- **The €5,000 threshold** comes from social security law (art. 44, paragraph 2, DL 269/2003). Above it, occasional earnings become subject to contributions to the INPS *Gestione Separata*, the INPS pension scheme that also covers occasional workers.

So you can go over €5,000 and still be an occasional worker, and you can need a Partita IVA well below €5,000 if your work has become habitual.

## What the €5,000 threshold really is

Up to €5,000 a year from occasional self-employed work, no INPS contributions are due. Above it, contributions are due **only on the excess**, the part above €5,000, not on the whole amount.

### What counts towards €5,000

- **All clients together.** The threshold is not per client: add up everything you receive for occasional work.
- **The calendar year**, from 1 January to 31 December.
- **The gross fees you were actually paid** (cash basis), before withholding and not just what you billed. A receipt issued in December but paid in January counts towards the following year.

<Callout type="info" title="Income tax is a separate matter">
The €5,000 threshold only concerns INPS. Income tax (IRPEF) applies to occasional income from the first euro.
</Callout>

## What to do when you go over €5,000

1. **Register with the INPS Gestione Separata.** You can apply online on the INPS website with SPID, CIE or CNS, through the service called Domanda Iscrizione Parasubordinati, or ask a *patronato* (a welfare advice centre) to do it for you.
2. **Tell your clients.** Give each client your total for the year, including the new receipt, so they can calculate the contributions. The €5,000 declaration on your receipt does exactly this.
3. **Add the INPS lines to your receipt.** Your one-third share is deducted from your payment; the client's two thirds is an extra cost on top of the gross fee.
4. **Let the client pay INPS.** The client withholds your share and pays the full contribution, its two thirds plus your third, to INPS.

Contributions are collected through clients that are withholding agents (*sostituti d'imposta*), such as Italian companies, entities and professionals.

<Callout type="warning" title="Mostly private or foreign clients?">
Private individuals and foreign clients don't act as withholding agents. If you go over €5,000 mainly with them, check your position with a CAF or patronato.
</Callout>

## The 2026 rates: 33.72% or 24%

| Your situation | INPS rate | Your share (1/3) | Client's share (2/3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No other mandatory pension coverage | 33.72% | 11.24% | 22.48% |
| Pensioner, or already insured elsewhere (for example as an employee) | 24% | 8% | 16% |

The rates for 2026 are set out in INPS circular n. 8/2026. Each rate applies only to the excess over €5,000, and the shares are always split two thirds to the client and one third to you.

## Worked example: crossing €5,000

You've already received €3,000 in 2026 for occasional work. You now do a €4,000 job for an Italian company. Your total reaches €7,000, so contributions are due on the €2,000 above the threshold. The withholding tax is still 20% of the gross fee.

| Item | 33.72% rate | 24% rate |
|---|---|---|
| Gross fee | €4,000 | €4,000 |
| Amount above €5,000 | €2,000 | €2,000 |
| Total INPS contribution | €674.40 | €480 |
| Your share (1/3) | €224.80 | €160 |
| Client's share (2/3) | €449.60 | €320 |
| Withholding tax (20% of gross) | €800 | €800 |
| Net to you | €2,975.20 | €3,040 |
| Total cost for the client | €4,449.60 | €4,320 |

The €2 stamp duty applies as usual and isn't included above. On the receipt, the €5,000 declaration states your total for the year (€7,000), the excess (€2,000), the rate and both shares.

<Callout type="tip" title="Later receipts in the same year">
Once you're over €5,000, any further receipt in the same calendar year is entirely above the threshold, so contributions apply to its full gross amount.
</Callout>

## Do you need a Partita IVA above €5,000?

Not because of the amount. Crossing €5,000 only triggers INPS contributions; it doesn't turn occasional work into a business. What matters is how you work: if the activity is habitual, continuous or organised, you need a Partita IVA at any income level.

That said, if you go over €5,000 every year with the same kind of job, ask yourself honestly whether the work is still occasional, and check with a commercialista. The [guide to working without a VAT number](/en/freelance-italy-without-vat/) lists the warning signs.

## Income tax: due from the first euro

Social security and income tax are separate. Whatever the amount:

- occasional income is *redditi diversi* (other income), taxed under IRPEF together with your other income;
- Italian withholding agents deduct 20% as an advance (art. 25 DPR 600/1973), which is credited in your 730 or Redditi PF return;
- private and foreign clients don't withhold, so you declare that income yourself;
- you can deduct expenses specifically incurred to produce the income (art. 71 TUIR).

Earning less than €5,000 does not make the income tax-free. ## FAQ

### Is €5,000 the maximum I can earn without a Partita IVA?

No. There is no income cap for occasional work. €5,000 is the INPS threshold; what requires a Partita IVA is habitual or organised work, whatever you earn.

### Is the €5,000 per client or in total?

In total. It covers everything you receive for occasional work from all clients in the same calendar year.

### Do I pay INPS on the whole amount once I go over?

No, only on the part above €5,000. In the example above, contributions are due on €2,000, not on €7,000.

### Who pays the INPS contributions?

The client pays two thirds and you pay one third, which the client withholds from your payment. The client then pays the whole contribution to INPS.

### Do I pay income tax if I earn less than €5,000?

Yes. The threshold only concerns social security. Income tax applies from the first euro, and you declare occasional income in your tax return.
